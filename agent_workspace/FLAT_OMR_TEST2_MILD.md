## Error inspection — Flat handles the degraded image dramatically worse

Checked measure-by-measure (not inferred from the aggregate): Flat read
**measures 1-3 of this piece exactly correctly** (note-for-note,
rhythm-for-rhythm identical to ground truth) — then, starting at measure
4, produces **zero corresponding content for measures 4 through 39**
(36 consecutive ground-truth measures with nothing aligned to them in
Flat's output), some content reappears compressed into measures 40-42,
and the final 6 real measures (43-48) never appear at all. Flat's own
output is internally consistent (a clean, sequentially-numbered 1-42
measure stream) — it did not produce garbage, it produced a
**structurally collapsed reading of the page**: a large middle section
of real content appears to have been lost or merged during measure/
staff-boundary detection under the mild rotation+warp+lighting
degradation.

This is the **same class of failure** this investigation already
root-caused for page-omr (structural/barline misreading under compound
geometric distortion, not symbol-recognition failure) — and it is worse
in absolute terms than page-omr's OWN pre-deskew average on this tier
(65.3% mild pitch accuracy across the corpus): Flat's 7.2% here is
below even raw, undeskewed page-omr's typical mild-tier performance.
Unlike page-omr, **Mediant has no deskew-equivalent fix available for a
third-party black-box API** — there is no preprocessing lever here the
way there was for page-omr.

**Direct answer to "does Flat handle degradation better or worse than
page-omr": worse, by a wide margin, on this test.**
